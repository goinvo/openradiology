# Top Problems in Radiology in the US
**Sources: peer-reviewed journals, professional society publications, government data**
*Last updated: June 2025*

---

## How to read this report

Each entry is a **distinct category** of problem. Where specific conditions (missed PE, missed breast cancer, etc.) appear, they serve as **evidence for the category** — not as separate problems. Each category is justified by the strength of its sourced evidence.

---

## 1. Diagnostic & Interpretive Error

**What it is:** Radiologists miss, misidentify, or misinterpret findings present in the image. This is the foundational failure mode of the field and underlies the majority of radiology malpractice claims.

**The evidence:**

- Real-time error rate averages **3–5%**; retrospective review reveals a **~30% discrepancy rate** (findings visible in hindsight but missed at the time). This rate has **not materially improved in 75 years** despite CT, MRI, PACS, and AI.
  > Itri JN et al. *RadioGraphics.* 2018;38(6):1845–1865. [doi:10.1148/rg.2018180021](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/rg.2018180021)

- Up to **84% of errors are perceptual** — the radiologist's eye passes over the abnormality without cognitive recognition. Error rates are "unchanged for 75 years."
  > Bruno MA et al. *RadioGraphics.* 2015;35(6):1668–1676. [doi:10.1148/rg.2015150023](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/rg.2015150023)

- **~75% of all malpractice lawsuits** against radiologists allege negligence related to diagnostic errors.
  > Berlin L. *AJR.* 2007;189(3):517–522. [doi:10.2214/AJR.07.2209](https://ajronline.org/doi/10.2214/AJR.07.2209)

**What the evidence looks like in practice** (specific conditions that illustrate the scale):
- **Lung cancer on chest X-ray:** 19–30% miss rate in clinical practice (*Chest* 1999; *Radiology* 2003)
- **Breast cancer on mammography:** False-negative rate rose from <1 to 2.5 per 1,000 screened women between 2010–2022, using the ACR National Mammography Database (*AJR* 2025)
- **Pulmonary embolism on CT-PA:** In one 6,769-study review, 53 missed PEs resulted in 14 patients developing subsequent new PEs (*AJR* 2014)
- **Early stroke on CT:** Systematic review of 35 studies found interobserver agreement κ <0.5 for early ischemic changes — poor even among trained readers (*Radiology* 2005)

**Why it's a top problem:** It affects every imaging modality, every subspecialty, and every practice setting. With 300M+ imaging studies performed in the US annually, a 4% real-time error rate represents millions of clinically significant misses per year — and the rate has not budged.

## 2. Communication & Follow-up Failures

**What it is:** Findings are correctly identified but not communicated to the treating team, or recommended follow-up is never completed. This is a distinct failure from interpretation — the radiologist did their job; the handoff broke.

**The evidence:**

- Approximately **75% of malpractice claims** against radiologists involve communication failures — not missed findings. Ineffective communication is the most-cited root cause in Joint Commission sentinel event analyses.
  > Berlin L. *Applied Radiology.* 2010. [appliedradiology.com](https://appliedradiology.com/articles/failure-of-radiologic-communication-an-increasing-cause-of-malpractice-litigation-and-harm-to-patients)

- In a 4-year prospective quality improvement study at a major academic center, even with active monitoring, **1.6–5% of critical results were not closed on time** — representing the ceiling of what best-practice systems achieve.
  > *Radiology.* 2011. [doi:10.1148/radiol.11101396](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.11101396)

- A 2024 JACR scoping review confirms there is still **no standardized definition of "critical findings"** across institutions, making consistent communication protocols impossible to enforce.
  > *JACR.* 2024. [S1546-1440(24)00773-7](https://www.jacr.org/article/S1546-1440(24)00773-7/fulltext)

- Fewer than **40% of ordering providers** adhere to radiologists' follow-up recommendations for incidental findings. **1 in 7 patients** with major radiologic abnormalities had no documented follow-up whatsoever.
  > Larson PA et al. *JACR.* 2011;8(3):180–185. [doi:10.1016/j.jacr.2010.07.013](https://www.jacr.org/article/S1546-1440(10)00284-X/fulltext)
  > The FIND Program. *Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine.* 2023. [doi:10.1007/s10278-023-00780-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-023-00780-6)

**Why it's a top problem:** Communication failures are the **#1 driver of radiology malpractice** — more than diagnostic error itself. The incidental finding follow-up problem alone affects tens of millions of patients annually (abdominal CT has a ~30–40% incidental finding rate). Both failures share the same structural root cause: no EHR-native system closes the loop between a radiology report and confirmed clinical action.

## 3. Radiation Safety

**What it is:** CT imaging exposes patients to ionizing radiation that carries a documented, population-level cancer risk. The harm is compounded by overutilization, wide dose variability between facilities, and cumulative exposure in patients who undergo repeated imaging.

**The evidence:**

- CT may account for approximately **1.5–2% of all US cancer cases annually**, based on 2007 scan volumes. With 80M+ CT scans now performed per year (up from 62M in 2007), the projected burden has only grown.
  > Brenner DJ, Hall EJ. *NEJM.* 2007;357(22):2277–2284. [doi:10.1056/NEJMra072149](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra072149)

- A study of 4 major academic medical centers found a **13-fold variation in radiation dose** for the same CT exam type, and estimated **~29,000 new cancers and 14,500 cancer deaths** attributable to CT annually.
  > Smith-Bindman R et al. *Archives of Internal Medicine.* 2009;169(22):2078–2086. [doi:10.1001/archinternmed.2009.427](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20008690/)

- In a 22-year cohort of 31,462 patients, **7% had accumulated enough CT radiation** to raise their estimated lifetime cancer risk by ≥1% above baseline.
  > Sodickson A et al. *Radiology.* 2009;251(1):175–184. [doi:10.1148/radiol.2511081296](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/radiol.2511081296)

**Why it's a top problem:** The 13-fold dose variation between facilities for the same exam is not a fringe finding — it reflects the absence of standardized dose optimization protocols across US radiology departments. Pediatric patients carry the highest lifetime risk due to greater radiosensitivity and longer remaining lifespan. CT use has continued to rise since these landmark studies were published.

## 4. Workforce Shortage & Burnout

**What it is:** The US has a growing, documented mismatch between the supply of radiologists and the demand for imaging services. The gap is structural, accelerating post-COVID, and directly affects diagnostic quality through fatigue and coverage gaps.

**The evidence:**

- Imaging demand is growing **~5% annually**; radiologist supply is growing **~2% annually**. Under current projections, demand will outpace supply through 2055.
  > Harvey L. Neiman Health Policy Institute. 2024. [neimanhpi.org](https://www.neimanhpi.org/press-releases/new-studies-shed-light-on-the-future-radiologist-workforce-shortage-by-projecting-future-radiologist-supply-and-demand-for-imaging/)

- **~50% of radiology job searches in 2023 went unfilled**; the average time to fill a full-time radiology position is **130 days**.
  > 2024 AAPPR Benchmarking Report, cited in Medicus HCS. [medicushcs.com](https://medicushcs.com/resources/navigating-the-radiologist-shortage)

- Diagnostic errors peak when daily case volume reaches **121% of a radiologist's normal workload**; analysis based on **2.9 million radiologic examinations**.
  > Waite S et al. *AJR.* 2017;208(4):739–749. [doi:10.2214/AJR.16.16963](https://ajronline.org/doi/10.2214/AJR.16.16963)

- A systematic review of **27 studies** confirmed fatigue reduces diagnostic accuracy — specifically fracture detection, oculomotor performance, and major discrepancy rates.
  > Stec N et al. *AJR.* 2018;210(4):799–806. [doi:10.2214/AJR.17.18613](https://ajronline.org/doi/10.2214/AJR.17.18613)

**Why it's a top problem:** Workforce shortage and burnout are not just operational concerns — they are diagnostic safety issues. Fatigue is a measurable, documented driver of the error rate in Problem 1. The pipeline constraints are structural (GME slot limits) and will not self-correct. The shortage also drives geographic disparities in Problem 5.

## 5. Access & Equity Disparities

**What it is:** Significant gaps exist in who can access imaging in the US — by geography, race, and socioeconomic status — and these gaps translate directly into later-stage diagnoses and worse clinical outcomes.

**The evidence:**

- Advanced imaging modalities (MRI, PET/CT) are **less available in hospitals serving communities of color**; when available, image quality and interpretation quality are lower. Disparities in mammography and lung cancer screening directly produce **later-stage disease at diagnosis** in underserved populations.
  > Waite S, Scott J, Colombo D. *Radiology.* 2021;299(1):27–35. [doi:10.1148/radiol.2021203742](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/radiol.2021203742)

- **Racial disparities in diagnostic delay** among women with breast cancer are documented; Black women and low-SES patients disproportionately present at later stages due in part to imaging access and follow-up gaps.
  > *JACR.* 2021. [doi:10.1016/j.jacr.2021.05.018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144021005494)

- Low socioeconomic status was the **only variable independently associated** with higher odds of appointment delays in oncology care including imaging.
  > *PMC.* 2025. [PMC12150564](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12150564/)

**Why it's a top problem:** Imaging disparities compound at every stage of the radiology cycle — access to the scanner, quality of image acquisition, quality of interpretation, and follow-up after findings. The Waite 2021 *Radiology* paper establishes all four as distinct failure points, not just the first. Stage at diagnosis is the strongest predictor of cancer survival, making imaging access a direct mortality issue.

## 6. AI & Technology Failures

**What it is:** FDA-cleared radiology AI tools are deployed without adequate real-world validation — particularly across demographic subgroups and equipment types. Bias in training data produces algorithms that fail silently on the patients who most need accurate diagnosis.

**The evidence:**

- Of 151 FDA-cleared imaging AI products reviewed: only **4% disclosed study participant demographics**, only **5.3% reported scanner specifications**, and only **34% had multi-institutional validation**. The majority have unknown performance across demographic subgroups and imaging equipment types.
  > *Clinical Radiology.* 2023;78(2):e13–e24. [doi:10.1016/j.crad.2022.09.122](https://www.clinicalradiologyonline.net/article/S0009-9260(22)00667-5/fulltext)

- Radiology AI models use **"shortcut learning"** — learning spurious features (e.g., presence of chest tubes, ICU portable markers) rather than true pathology. One FDA-cleared algorithm misclassified ischemic stroke as intracranial hemorrhage.
  > Banerjee I et al. *JACR.* 2023;20(9):842–851. [PMC11192466](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11192466/)

- FDA clearance does not validate real-world performance. Disparate outputs for demographic subgroups remain a **critical unresolved limitation** of cleared radiology AI.
  > Kohli M et al. *npj Digital Medicine.* 2022. [PMC8903084](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8903084/)

**Why it's a top problem:** FDA clearance is widely interpreted as a safety signal. The evidence shows it is not sufficient. Most cleared tools were trained on homogeneous datasets — meaning their real-world performance in diverse populations is unknown. Shortcut bias is especially dangerous because algorithms produce high-confidence wrong predictions in edge cases, exactly where human oversight is already strained.

## 7. Administrative Burden & Reimbursement Pressure

**What it is:** Radiology practices operate under sustained financial and administrative pressure — from prior authorization overhead to declining Medicare reimbursement — that strains capacity, accelerates workforce exits, and creates barriers to patient access.

**The evidence:**

- Prior authorization accounts for an estimated **$35 billion in US healthcare administrative spending** annually. Physicians complete an average of **39 prior authorization requests per week**, spending **13 hours/week** — combined staff time equivalent to **100,000+ full-time registered nurses per year**.
  > PMC, survey Apr–Jun 2023. *Published 2024.* [PMC11425057](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11425057/)
  > AMA. [ama-assn.org](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way)

- **81.7% of appealed Medicare Advantage prior authorization denials were fully or partially overturned** — meaning most initial denials were incorrect, but only practices that appeal discover this.
  > PMC 2024, citing CMS Medicare Advantage data. [PMC11425057](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11425057/)

- Medicare's 2025 payment schedule includes a **2.8% cut to the conversion factor**. Radiologists performed **13% more RVUs per Medicare beneficiary in 2021 vs. 2005** — significantly more work for proportionally less pay. Declining reimbursement is identified as one of the seven most pressing "wicked problems" facing radiology.
  > Siewert B et al. *JACR.* 2024. (ACR Intersociety Meeting report) [radiologybusiness.com](https://radiologybusiness.com)

**Why it's a top problem:** Administrative burden and reimbursement cuts directly reduce practice capacity — accelerating the workforce shortage in Problem 4, discouraging investment in technology, and creating access barriers that worsen Problem 5. The 81.7% overturn rate on appealed denials is a damning figure: the system is spending billions processing decisions that are, by majority, incorrect.
