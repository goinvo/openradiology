# Research Workflow

A plain-English recipe for producing thorough, structured research reports on any topic.

---

## Phase 1 — Intake (Ask Before Researching)

Before doing any research, ask the user these questions. Do not skip ahead until all are answered.

1. **Topic scope** — How broad or narrow should the research be? Are there specific subtopics to include or exclude?
2. **Audience** — Who will read this report? (e.g., yourself, a team, a client, a non-expert, an executive)
3. **Depth** — Quick overview or deep dive?
4. **Specific questions** — Are there particular questions this report must answer?
5. **Format preference** — Any preferences on length, use of tables, or level of detail?
6. **Use case** — What will this report be used for? (informs tone and framing)

Rule: Do not begin research until all intake questions are answered.

---

## Phase 2 — Research

Search broadly first, then drill into specific subtopics. Gather findings across these areas (as relevant to the topic):

- Background and context — what it is, where it came from
- Key players, concepts, or components
- How it works in practice
- Recent developments or trends
- Open questions, debates, or known unknowns

Note the source for each key finding (domain + title at minimum).

---

## Phase 3 — Organize

Before writing the report:

- Group findings into logical sections based on the topic and what the audience needs
- Cut anything that falls outside the agreed scope
- Flag any gaps where information was sparse, conflicting, or unavailable

---

## Phase 4 — Write the Report

Produce a structured markdown report using this template:

```
# [Topic] — Research Report

## Executive Summary
One paragraph. What this report covers and the single most important thing to know.

## [Section 1 — based on topic]
...

## [Section 2]
...

## [Additional sections as needed]
...

## Key Takeaways
- Bullet list of the most important insights from the research

## Recommended Next Steps
- Concrete actions the reader should consider based on the findings

## Sources
- [Source title or description] — domain/URL
- ...
```

Formatting rules:
- Prefer bullet points over dense paragraphs
- Use tables for comparisons or structured data
- Keep section headers clear and jargon-free
- Tone should match the audience defined in intake

Save the finished report to: `output/[topic-slug]-research.md`
